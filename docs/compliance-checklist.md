# UK Short-Term Let Compliance Checklist

> Last checked: **2026-11-16**. Every item below is traceable to a record in this repository's `data/` files (record IDs in brackets) or to an official source linked beside it. A figure marked **not published** means the official source has not published it — we do not estimate. **This checklist is general information only, not legal advice.** Rules change; confirm each item against the official source before acting.

Tick items off as you complete them. Items are grouped by jurisdiction — work through the section for your property's nation, then the general safety section.

## Scotland — mandatory council licence

Licensing has been mandatory across Scotland since 1 October 2022. You must hold a licence before taking a booking ([`scotland-councils.json` — `meta.national_framework`](data/scotland-councils.json)).

- [ ] **Hold a council short-term let licence before taking bookings or receiving guests** — operating without one is a criminal offence, fine up to £2,500, plus a possible 1-year ban on reapplying. Source: [gov.scot — Short-term lets](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/)
- [ ] **Apply to your own council's licensing team** — each of Scotland's 32 councils runs its own process with its own fees. Find your council's fee range and source in [`data/scotland-councils.json`](data/scotland-councils.json) (fee unpublished for East Lothian and North Lanarkshire — check the council directly)
- [ ] **Check whether you are in a short-term let control area** — only two are designated: City of Edinburgh (whole council area, since 5 September 2022) and Highland Council — Badenoch and Strathspey ward (since 4 March 2024). In a control area, planning permission for the change of use must be applied for or granted before a licence application. Source: [gov.scot — Short-term lets](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/)
- [ ] **Outside a control area, check whether planning permission is still needed** — it may be required case-by-case if the let is a material change of use. Ask your council's planning team before applying ([`scotland-councils.json` — `planning_note` on your council's record](data/scotland-councils.json))
- [ ] **Know your licence length** — a newly issued secondary letting licence lasts 1 year; on renewal it may be 1 year or 3 years. The other three types (home sharing, home letting, home letting and home sharing) last 3 years. Source: [directbook.uk — Scotland short-term let licence](https://directbook.uk/scotland-short-term-let-licence/) (citing City of Edinburgh Council short-term lets policy, updated May 2025)
- [ ] **Meet the mandatory safety conditions** — fire risk assessment; working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms; furnishings meeting fire safety regulations; annual gas safety check by a Gas Safe registered engineer; electrical safety inspection at least every five years with an Electrical Installation Condition Report and portable appliance testing; Legionella risk assessment; meeting the repairing standard; valid Energy Performance Certificate issued within the last 10 years; buildings insurance plus public liability insurance. Source: [Scottish Government licensing scheme guidance part 1, section 2](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-licensing-scheme-part-1-guidance-hosts-operators/)
- [ ] **Apply well ahead of your first guests** — the council must decide within 9 months of receiving a complete application. Source: [Scottish Government licensing scheme guidance part 1](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-licensing-scheme-part-1-guidance-hosts-operators/)
- [ ] **Display your licence number** on all listings and on your own website once granted
- [ ] **Temporary exemptions (if relevant)** — some councils offer up to three exemptions per calendar year, not exceeding six weeks in total, often on a shorter form at a lower fee; availability depends on your council's published policy. Source: [Scottish Government licensing scheme guidance part 1, section 1](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-licensing-scheme-part-1-guidance-hosts-operators/)

## Wales — free registration now, licensing later

- [ ] **Register with the Welsh Revenue Authority (WRA) by 31 March 2027** if your accommodation is open for bookings before 1 April 2027 — registration is free and mandatory across Wales, even where the local council has no visitor levy. Source: [GOV.WALES — Failing to register, update details, or deregister](https://www.gov.wales/failing-register-update-details-or-deregister-your-visitor-accommodation) [`wales-registration-deadline`]
- [ ] **Check whether you must register** — you must register if you take bookings for overnight stays in visitor accommodation in Wales, are responsible for the accommodation, and your bookings are for 31 nights or less. Source: [GOV.WALES — Registering visitor accommodation overview](https://www.gov.wales/registering-visitor-accommodation-overview) [`wales-registration-scope`]
- [ ] **New accommodation opening on or after 1 April 2027: register before opening for bookings.** Source: [GOV.WALES — Failing to register, update details, or deregister](https://www.gov.wales/failing-register-update-details-or-deregister-your-visitor-accommodation) [`wales-registration-new-after-apr-2027`]
- [ ] **Update your registration details or deregister within 30 days of any change.** Source: [GOV.WALES — Failing to register, update details, or deregister](https://www.gov.wales/failing-register-update-details-or-deregister-your-visitor-accommodation) [`wales-registration-new-after-apr-2027`]
- [ ] **Know the penalties** — £100 per premises for late registration, update or deregistration, payable within 30 days; then £10 per premises per day for up to 30 days, then a further £1,000 per premises. Maximum £1,400 per premises. Source: [GOV.WALES — Failing to register, update details, or deregister](https://www.gov.wales/failing-register-update-details-or-deregister-your-visitor-accommodation) [`wales-penalty-late-registration`, `wales-penalty-escalation`]
- [ ] **Check whether your council charges the visitor levy** — each of the 22 Welsh councils decides for itself after consulting its community, with 12 months' notice; a levy takes effect on 1 April or 1 October, earliest 1 April 2027. Cardiff is the first council charging it (stays from 1 April 2027). Source: [GOV.WALES — Senedd passes legislation to support the future of tourism in Wales](https://www.gov.wales/senedd-passes-legislation-to-support-the-future-of-tourism-in-wales) [`wales-visitor-levy-council-choice`]
- [ ] **If a levy applies, know the rates** — £1.30 per person per night (standard, including self-catering lets, hotels and B&Bs); 75p per person per night for shared rooms in hostels, bunkhouses, bothies or dormitories and for tent pitches. Under-18s are exempt on the lower rate only; stays of 32 nights or more in one booking pay no levy. Source: [GOV.WALES — Senedd passes legislation to support the future of tourism in Wales](https://www.gov.wales/senedd-passes-legislation-to-support-the-future-of-tourism-in-wales) [`wales-visitor-levy-rates`]
- [ ] **Plan for licensing (not yet in force)** — a licensing scheme is likely to start in 2029 (government expectation, not a statutory date); the legal default is 4 March 2030. No licence is needed yet and no application exists. Expected standards include gas and electrical safety certificates, carbon monoxide alarms, public liability insurance, and a fire safety risk assessment. Source: [GOV.WALES — Licensing visitor accommodation in Wales](https://www.gov.wales/licensing-visitor-accommodation-wales) [`wales-licensing-expected-2029`, `wales-licensing-legal-default-2030`]
- [ ] **Licensing fee: not published** — no official fee has been published as of 2026-11-16. Do not rely on figures in press reports. [`wales-licensing-fee`]
- [ ] **Check your planning use class** — Wales has C3 (sole or main residence), C5 (second home) and C6 (short-term let). Changing between them is generally permitted development and needs no planning permission, unless your local planning authority has made an Article 4 direction removing that right — check with your council. Source: [Town and Country Planning (Use Classes) (Amendment) (Wales) Order 2022](https://assets.caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/admin/2025/437/ewhc_admin_2025_437.pdf) [`wales-planning-use-classes`, `wales-planning-c3-c6-change`]
- [ ] **Check the business rates tests** — a self-catering property is non-domestic (business rates, not council tax) only if available for commercial letting for 252 days or more and actually let for at least 182 days in the previous 12 months. From 1 April 2026, an average over the previous 2 or 3 years may satisfy the 182-day test, and up to 14 charitable days per year may count. Source: [Business Wales — Non-domestic rates: self-catering properties](https://businesswales.gov.wales/non-domestic-rates-self-catering-properties-wales) [`wales-business-rates-252-182`, `wales-business-rates-averaging`]
- [ ] **Watch the 182-day consultation outcome** — the Welsh Government consulted 31 July – 23 October 2026 on changes to the letting criteria; as of 2026-11-16 no government response had been published. Source: [GOV.WALES — Written statement: consultation on classification of self-catering properties](https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-consultation-classification-self-catering-properties-local-tax-purposes) [`wales-business-rates-consultation`]
- [ ] **Know the council tax fallback** — a property that fails the 252/182-day tests falls back to council tax, and Welsh local authorities may set a second-home council tax premium of up to 300%. Source: [strsoftware.co.uk — Wales 182-day rule](https://www.strsoftware.co.uk/regulations/wales/182-day-rule/) [`wales-council-tax-premium`]

## England — national register coming, not yet open

- [ ] **Know the register status: expected March 2027, not yet open.** There is currently no register to join, no portal, no published fee and no exact start date. The March 2027 date is an expectation, not a fixed statutory date. Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations) [`england-register-status`]
- [ ] **Registration fee: not published.** [`england-register-fee`]
- [ ] **Penalties for failing to register: not published.** The 2023 Act allows regulations to set civil penalties, but no amount has been decided. [`england-register-penalties`]
- [ ] **Operator, exemptions and what registration will require: not published.** [`england-register-operator`]
- [ ] **Greater London: respect the 90-night cap** — up to 90 nights per calendar year without planning permission, provided you are liable for council tax at the premises. Beyond 90 nights (or if the council tax condition fails), planning permission for a material change of use is required. Source: [Deregulation Act 2015, s.44](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/20/section/44/enacted) [`england-london-90-night`, `england-london-beyond-90`]
- [ ] **Outside London: ask your local planning authority** — it decides case-by-case whether you need planning permission, based on how the property is used and its impact on neighbours and the local area. There is no national night threshold outside Greater London. Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations) [`england-planning-case-by-case`]
- [ ] **Do not rely on a C5 use class in England** — a new C5 class for short-term lets was consulted on in 2023 but is not law; no statutory instrument has been laid. (In Wales, C5 means a second home and C6 is the short-term let class.) [`england-planning-c5-proposal`]
- [ ] **Check the business rates tests** — since 1 April 2023, a self-catering property in England is rated for business rates (rather than council tax) where it was available to let commercially for 140 nights in a 12-month period, actually let commercially for 70 nights in that period, and the owner intends to keep it available for at least 140 nights in the next year. [`england-business-rates-140-70`]
- [ ] **Tax: the Furnished Holiday Lettings regime was abolished on 6 April 2025** — from the 2025 to 2026 tax year onwards, income from short-term holiday accommodation is taxed under the usual residential landlord rules; the previous FHL tax reliefs no longer apply. Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations) [`england-tax-fhl-abolished`]

## General safety (England — directly checked; see notes)

These items are summarised from the official England pages in [`docs/safety-requirements.md`](safety-requirements.md). Scotland and Wales have their own safety legislation — for Scotland, see the mandatory conditions in the Scotland section above; for Wales, the licensing safety standards are expected but not yet in force.

- [ ] **Fire risk assessment** — carry out a suitable and sufficient fire risk assessment and record it in writing; review it regularly (usually annually) and after any significant change or fire. Applies to paying-guest accommodation even for a single night, under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005. Source: [Home Office — Making your small paying guest accommodation safe from fire](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-your-small-paying-guest-accommodation-safe-from-fire/making-your-small-paying-guest-accommodation-safe-from-fire-accessible)
- [ ] **Annual gas safety check** — if you provide gas appliances, a Gas Safe registered engineer must check them at least every 12 months; keep the record for 2 years and give a copy to existing guests within 28 days of the check. The same duties apply to LPG appliances. Source: [HSE — Gas safety: landlords' FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/faqlandlord.htm)
- [ ] **Electrical safety** — have the fixed electrical installation inspected and tested at least every five years, and the wiring checked by a qualified contractor; only competent electrical contractors may carry out electrical work. Source: [Home Office — Making your small paying guest accommodation safe from fire](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-your-small-paying-guest-accommodation-safe-from-fire/making-your-small-paying-guest-accommodation-safe-from-fire-accessible)
- [ ] **Insurance** — hold dedicated holiday-let insurance, public liability cover, and building and contents cover suitable for short-term letting. GOV.UK frames this as advice ("you should have"), not a legal "must". Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations)
- [ ] **Check whether you need an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)** — GOV.UK: "You must check whether you need an EPC." Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations)
- [ ] **Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms** — follow the HSE smoke and carbon monoxide safety guidance linked from the GOV.UK short-term let guide. Exact placement, numbers and interlinking requirements were not directly checked in this repository — confirm against the HSE guidance. Source: [GOV.UK — Letting out a self-catering holiday home in England](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations)
